{{ t.aboutTitle }}

Panamá vive aquí. We’re building the answer to a question thousands of Panamanians ask every time they move to a new American city: where is everybody?

Our mission
Unite, empower, and celebrate

PanaHub exists to unite, empower, and celebrate the Panamanian community across the United States — one trusted platform for connection, opportunity, culture, and growth.

We’re a small community with a big diaspora, spread thin across dozens of states. The businesses, associations, and events exist — they’re just invisible unless you already know someone. PanaHub makes them findable.

What we’re solving
Disconnection, misinformation, loneliness, and a lack of accessible resources — especially for newcomers and for second-generation Panamanian-Americans looking to reconnect with their heritage.
Who it’s for
Panamanians, Panamanian-Americans, and anyone connected to Panama living in the United States — from someone who landed last week to a family three generations in.
Verification
How we confirm what’s on here

A directory is only worth as much as its accuracy. We never label a business as Panamanian-owned just because its name contains “Panama” or because it serves Latin food. Every listing carries one of these statuses:

✓ Verified Panamanian
Credible information confirms the owner, founder, or organization has a genuine Panamanian connection. Listings carry a last-verified date.
Verification Pending
We have a credible lead but haven’t confirmed ownership yet. Shown honestly rather than hidden or overstated.
Official Government Resource
Embassies and consulates. These never receive an ownership badge — they’re government services, not businesses.
This gets built by the community

Every listing on PanaHub came from someone who knew about it. If we’re missing a business, an association, an event, or a scholarship — tell us.

If you only learn one thing
Panama was a crossing long before it was a canal.

Every wave that passed through the isthmus — Spanish, African, Caribbean, Chinese, Indigenous, American — stayed and left something in the food, the music, and the language. That layering isn't a footnote to Panamanian identity. It is Panamanian identity.

1855 — the railroad
The first transcontinental railroad in the Americas drew workers from the Caribbean, China, and Europe.
17th century — the Congos
Africans escaping colonial plantations founded free communities near Portobelo. The tradition is still danced today.
1999 — the handover
Panama took full control of the canal, closing a century-long chapter of foreign administration.
For parents & teachers

Every track is written to be read aloud or assigned. Use it for heritage nights, Spanish class, or a kid asking questions you weren't taught the answers to.
